Five checks before you scale a Meta campaign will save more money than a new audience trick. Scaling means giving a campaign more budget because the result you asked for is actually happening. It does not mean doubling spend because a dashboard looked busy on Tuesday. These checks are the ones we use before a budget moves. They do not manufacture a return figure. Meta does not owe you one, and neither do we.

:::takeaways
- Name the result before launch. A message, a follow, a visit, or a sale a person counted outside Ads Manager.
- Keep the ad account in the client’s name and read the export yourself.
- Meta’s learning note is about 50 results in the week after a significant edit. Small budgets often miss it. Do not “fix” that with daily edits.
- Change one thing at a time.
- Client-reported sales, where we state them, are about 17–20 per messaging campaign on average. That is not ROAS.
:::

## Check 1: name the result before anyone is charged

Write the result in a sentence a cashier could understand. “Messages about the blue size” is a result. “Engagement” is a mood. “ROI” is a ratio you cannot see until costs and income are both real.

Ads Manager will optimise toward the event you pick. If you pick landing page views, you will get page views from people who may never buy. If you pick purchases, and you sell two items a week, the ad set may sit in learning because it cannot gather the results Meta describes. [The learning phase help page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700) says exit usually follows about 50 results in the week after the last significant edit. Pick an event you can actually receive, or accept that learning will not finish and judge earlier signals on purpose. The [Shopify budget article](/blog/shopify-meta-ads-budget) walks through that trade-off with the other published numbers.

Do not relabel the column later. If the month was messages, the report is about messages. A sale counted in a notebook can sit beside it, labelled as the client’s count.

## Check 2: the account is theirs

The page, the ad account, the pixel or dataset, and the payment method should belong to the business. A manager can be an admin. A manager should not be the only person who can download history.

This is dull until the relationship ends. Then it is the whole argument. Marketivity’s [Meta Ads](/services/meta-ads) work is scoped so the client can open Ads Manager and see the same rows we see. The fee is separate from the spend. In taka that fee is ৳8,000, ৳15,000 or from ৳25,000 a month depending on the tier. Dollars and pounds are on the [pricing page](/pricing). None of those numbers is your media spend.

If an agency cannot add you as admin, do not scale. You would be scaling a black box.

## Check 3: you can see a week, not a vibe

A single afternoon is noise. Meta’s own budget rules make a single afternoon even noisier. The [budget documentation](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/bidding/overview/budgets/) says a daily budget is an average, and spend on a given day can be up to about 25% higher when the system sees opportunity. Their example is $10 becoming $12.50. It is supposed to balance over the week. Scaling because Tuesday overspent, or cutting because Wednesday underspent, fights that design.

Read seven days. Note the last significant edit. If you changed the creative yesterday, those seven days are not a clean read. Wait.

For costs in Bangladesh, do not import a US CPM and do not average three blogs into a fake official rate. The [BDT cost piece](/blog/facebook-ad-cost-bangladesh) shows why the published bands disagree. Your export replaces them.

## Check 4: one change, then quiet

Significant edits restart the learning conversation. A new audience, a new optimisation event, a large budget jump and a new creative on the same day give you nothing to learn from. You will have a story either way. You will not have a cause.

Pick the weakest ad and pause it, or raise the budget modestly, or replace one image. Then leave the rest. Talk Shop and others suggest small budget steps in their Shopify playbooks. Meta does not publish “raise by 20%” as a universal rule in the learning article. Treat any percentage step you read on a blog as that blog’s habit. The principle you can take from Meta is simpler: big edits reset the clock.

Write down what you changed and the date. Future you, and the client, will not remember.

## Check 5: do not upgrade a metric into a sale

A follow is not a customer. A click is not an order. A messaging thread is not a sale until money or a confirmed booking exists. If the client counts those sales, say “client-reported”.

The only figure of that kind we publish is client-reported: about 17–20 sales per messaging campaign on average, on the campaigns where the client did the counting. It is not revenue. It is not a return on ad spend. It is not a target we will print on your invoice. If your campaign is optimised for purchases on a website, it is the wrong comparison entirely.

When someone asks for ROI tips, this is the tip: build a two-column note. Column one is the Ads Manager result. Column two is money the business actually took, if someone counted it. The ratio, if you insist on one, uses column two and the real spend, and it belongs in a private note. It does not belong in an ad.

## What scaling looks like after the checks

You raise budget when column one is the result you wanted, the account is yours, the week is cleaner than the edit history, and you know what you are about to change. You do not raise budget to end an argument in a group chat.

Creative still matters. Three clear ads beat one vague ad. “Clear” means the product, the price or the offer, and the next step. It does not mean five fonts and a stock handshake. For a local business, say the area. For a shop, send people to the product that is in stock.

Tracking has to match the result. If the result is a purchase, the site has to report the purchase. If the result is a message, you do not need a perfect purchase pixel to judge the month, but you do need a person reading the inbox. Setting up a pixel you never check is not check 5. It is decoration.

## When to stop instead of scale

Stop or rebuild when the offer is wrong, the page price is wrong, or the replies are slower than the customer. More budget makes those problems louder. Also stop if the only winning ad depends on a claim you cannot repeat next week. Scaling a one-day stunt trains the account on a stunt.
